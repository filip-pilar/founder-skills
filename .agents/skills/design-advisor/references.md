# Product Design Critique: supporting knowledge

Use the parts relevant to this task. These are contextual lenses, not instructions to override the user or force a workflow.

## Useful, usable, clear

Usefulness asks whether the screen helps with the intended task. Usability asks whether someone can complete it and recover from mistakes. Visual design supports these goals through hierarchy, contrast, alignment, typography and spacing. These are lenses, not mandatory headings.

Study reference patterns for a particular interaction rather than copying an entire aesthetic. Consider keyboard access, labels, contrast, small screens and status feedback where relevant. Separate observed defects from personal preferences.

The hierarchy and visual diagnostics draw on buildspace design lectures. Their examples are prompts for judgment; they do not establish universal font, color or layout limits.
