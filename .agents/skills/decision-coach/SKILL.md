# Founder Decisions

Identify the actual decision and what success means to this founder. Separate facts, estimates, preferences and constraints. Consider meaningful alternatives, reversibility, downside and opportunity cost without inventing probabilities. Recommend an option and explain what evidence could change the recommendation.

When someone is stuck, locate a concrete obstacle rather than assigning a psychological diagnosis. Ask about missing constraints if necessary; avoid claiming to know an emotion or motive the user has not supplied. A small reversible step can be a better outcome than an exhaustive analysis.

For an explicitly requested weekly review, adapt to the user's time and context: examine meaningful changes, the current bottleneck and the next commitment. No compulsory seven-stage ritual. Do not turn an ordinary decision request into a recurring review.

Read [references.md](references.md) when a decision lens would help and [example.md](example.md) for a bounded recommendation. Finish with a recommendation or state precisely which missing evidence prevents one, while completing independent analysis. Keep authorization for execution separate from advice.
