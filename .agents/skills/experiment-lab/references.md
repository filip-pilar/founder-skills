## Evidence before tempo

Define what would change the decision before collecting results. Fast iteration is useful when the observation window supports it. A retention test needs time for return behavior; a low-traffic conversion test may need a different research method.

The single-variable heuristic helps attribute effects in controlled comparisons. It does not make sequential social posts or different weekly traffic equivalent to random assignment. Keep exploratory and causal claims distinct.

For small samples, report counts and uncertainty without asserting that a higher percentage proves improvement. Qualitative observation can reveal a concrete usability failure even when an A/B estimate is inconclusive.
